## Hemoglobin
aliases: Hemoglobin, Haemoglobin, Hb, Hgb
Hemoglobin is a protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen around the body.
If low: A low value can make a person feel tired, weak, dizzy, pale or short of breath. There are several possible reasons that only a doctor can work out.
If high: A high value is less common. A doctor looks for the reason, which can depend on things such as altitude, smoking or body water levels.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting or a very fast heartbeat.

## Red blood cell count
aliases: RBC, Red Blood Cells, RBC Count, Erythrocytes
This test counts the red blood cells, the cells that carry oxygen. It is read together with hemoglobin and the red cell indices.
If low: A low count can go with tiredness, weakness or breathlessness.
If high: A high count is read together with hemoglobin; a doctor looks for the reason.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, severe breathlessness or fainting.

## Hematocrit
aliases: Hematocrit, Haematocrit, PCV, Packed Cell Volume, HCT
Hematocrit (PCV) shows what share of the blood is made up of red blood cells. It is read together with hemoglobin.
If low: A low value can go with tiredness, weakness or breathlessness.
If high: A high value is read together with hemoglobin and the red cell count; a doctor looks for the reason.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, severe breathlessness or fainting.

## MCV, MCH and MCHC
aliases: MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW, red cell indices
These are red cell indices. They describe the average size of red blood cells, how much hemoglobin each carries, and how much the sizes vary. Doctors use them together with hemoglobin to understand blood test patterns.
If low: These values do not usually cause symptoms on their own; they guide the doctor in finding the reason for a hemoglobin result.
If high: These values do not usually cause symptoms on their own; they guide the doctor in finding the reason for a hemoglobin result.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, severe breathlessness or fainting.

## White blood cell count
aliases: WBC, TLC, Total Leucocyte Count, Total WBC Count, Leukocytes, White Blood Cells
White blood cells are part of the body's defence system. This test counts them.
If low: A low count can mean the body is less able to fight germs, so fever or repeated illness should be reported to a doctor promptly.
If high: A high count is often seen when the body is responding to illness, stress or some medicines. A doctor finds the reason using the symptoms and other tests.
Urgent symptoms: high fever, severe weakness, trouble breathing or confusion.

## Differential count
aliases: Neutrophils, Lymphocytes, Monocytes, Eosinophils, Basophils, Differential Leucocyte Count, DLC
The differential count shows the share of each type of white blood cell. Each type has a different job in the body's defence system.
If low: A doctor reads a low or high share together with the total white cell count and the symptoms; one share alone does not explain a cause.
If high: A doctor reads a low or high share together with the total white cell count and the symptoms; one share alone does not explain a cause.
Urgent symptoms: high fever, severe weakness, trouble breathing or confusion.

## Platelet count
aliases: Platelets, Platelet Count, PLT, Thrombocytes
Platelets are small blood cells that help the blood to clot.
If low: A low count can cause easy bruising, nosebleeds, bleeding gums or tiny red spots on the skin.
If high: A high count usually causes no symptoms by itself; a doctor looks for the reason.
Urgent symptoms: bleeding that does not stop, blood in vomit, urine or stool, black stools, a sudden severe headache or large unexplained bruises.

## ESR
aliases: ESR, Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate
ESR measures how fast red blood cells settle in a tube of blood. It is a general test that is not specific to one condition.
If low: A low value is usually not important by itself; a doctor reads it with other results.
If high: A raised value shows that something may be going on in the body, but not what. A doctor interprets it with the symptoms and other tests.
Urgent symptoms: persistent fever, unexplained weight loss, severe pain or trouble breathing.

## Fasting blood glucose
aliases: Glucose, Blood Sugar, Fasting Glucose, FBS, Glucose Fasting, Glucose Random, Glucose PP, Post Prandial Glucose
This test measures the amount of sugar (glucose) in the blood. Reference ranges depend on whether the sample was taken fasting or after a meal, so the range printed in the report for that sample type is the one to use.
If low: A low value can cause sweating, shaking, hunger, headache or confusion.
If high: A high value can cause strong thirst, passing urine often, tiredness or blurred vision.
Urgent symptoms: confusion, fainting, fits, fast breathing, vomiting that does not stop, or breath that smells fruity.

## HbA1c
aliases: HbA1c, Glycosylated Hemoglobin, Glycated Hemoglobin, A1c
HbA1c reflects the average blood sugar level over the previous few months.
If low: A low value is uncommon; a doctor reviews it with the other results.
If high: A high value means blood sugar has been higher than the usual limits on average over recent months. The doctor decides what this means and what to do.
Urgent symptoms: confusion, fainting, fast breathing or vomiting that does not stop.

## Total cholesterol
aliases: Cholesterol, Total Cholesterol
Cholesterol is a fatty substance in the blood. The total cholesterol result is read together with LDL, HDL and triglycerides.
If low: A low total value is read together with the other lipid tests by a doctor.
If high: A high value usually causes no symptoms. It is one of several factors a doctor considers for the health of the heart and blood vessels.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, pain spreading to the arm or jaw, sudden breathlessness, sudden weakness on one side of the body or trouble speaking.

## LDL cholesterol
aliases: LDL, LDL Cholesterol, Low Density Lipoprotein, VLDL
LDL and VLDL are types of cholesterol carriers in the blood. Doctors look at them together with the full lipid profile.
If low: A low value is read together with the full lipid profile by a doctor.
If high: A high value usually causes no symptoms. It is one of several factors a doctor considers for the health of the heart and blood vessels.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, pain spreading to the arm or jaw, sudden breathlessness, sudden weakness on one side of the body or trouble speaking.

## HDL cholesterol
aliases: HDL, HDL Cholesterol, High Density Lipoprotein
HDL is a type of cholesterol carrier in the blood. It is read together with the full lipid profile.
If low: A low value usually causes no symptoms. Doctors pay attention to it as one factor for the health of the heart and blood vessels.
If high: A high value is read together with the full lipid profile by a doctor.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, pain spreading to the arm or jaw, sudden breathlessness, sudden weakness on one side of the body or trouble speaking.

## Triglycerides
aliases: Triglycerides, TG
Triglycerides are a type of fat found in the blood. The result can be affected by whether the person had eaten before the sample was taken.
If low: A low value is read together with the full lipid profile by a doctor.
If high: A high value usually causes no symptoms. Very high values can sometimes cause severe stomach pain. A doctor reads it with the full lipid profile.
Urgent symptoms: severe stomach pain that does not go away, or chest pain.

## Creatinine
aliases: Creatinine, Serum Creatinine, eGFR
Creatinine is a waste product that the kidneys filter out of the blood. Doctors use it as one measure of how well the kidneys are working.
If low: A low value often reflects low muscle mass and is read together with other results by a doctor.
If high: A high value can mean the kidneys are filtering less well, but it can also rise for a short time, for example when the body is short of water or with some medicines. A doctor decides which applies.
Urgent symptoms: passing very little or no urine, swelling of the face or legs, severe vomiting, confusion or breathlessness.

## Urea and BUN
aliases: Urea, Blood Urea, Blood Urea (URIASE), BUN, Blood Urea Nitrogen
Urea is a waste product that the kidneys remove from the blood. It is read together with creatinine and other kidney tests.
If low: A low value is read with the other results by a doctor.
If high: A high value can be seen when the kidneys are filtering less well, when the body is short of water, or after a high-protein meal. A doctor decides which applies.
Urgent symptoms: passing very little or no urine, swelling of the face or legs, severe vomiting, confusion or breathlessness.

## Uric acid
aliases: Uric Acid, Serum Uric Acid
Uric acid is a waste product found in the blood.
If low: A low value is usually read together with other results by a doctor.
If high: A high value can sometimes lead to painful, swollen joints, but many people with a high value have no symptoms.
Urgent symptoms: a very painful, hot, swollen joint with fever.

## Liver enzymes
aliases: ALT, SGPT, AST, SGOT, ALP, Alkaline Phosphatase, GGT, SGPT (ALT), SGOT (AST)
ALT (SGPT), AST (SGOT), ALP and GGT are enzymes found in the liver and some other organs. Doctors read them together as part of a liver panel.
If low: A low value is usually not important by itself.
If high: A high value means these enzymes have leaked into the blood, which can happen for many reasons, including medicines, alcohol and illnesses. A doctor works out the reason.
Urgent symptoms: yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, pale stools, severe belly pain, vomiting blood, confusion or unusual drowsiness.

## Bilirubin
aliases: Bilirubin, Total Bilirubin, Direct Bilirubin, Indirect Bilirubin
Bilirubin is a yellow substance made when old red blood cells are broken down. It is processed by the liver.
If low: A low value is usually not important by itself.
If high: A high value can turn the skin and the whites of the eyes yellow and make urine dark. A doctor works out the reason using the other liver tests.
Urgent symptoms: yellow eyes or skin with severe belly pain, fever, confusion or vomiting blood.

## Albumin and proteins
aliases: Albumin, Total Protein, Globulin, A/G Ratio, Serum Albumin
Albumin and globulin are proteins in the blood. They are measured as part of liver and general health panels.
If low: A low value can go with swelling of the feet or legs. A doctor looks for the reason.
If high: A high value is often linked with the body being short of water. A doctor reads it with other results.
Urgent symptoms: swelling with breathlessness, or sudden swelling of the face.

## TSH
aliases: TSH, Thyroid Stimulating Hormone
TSH is a hormone made in the brain that tells the thyroid gland how much hormone to make. It is usually read together with T3 and T4.
If low: A low value can go with a racing heart, sweating, shaking, weight loss or feeling hot. The doctor decides what it means with T3 and T4.
If high: A high value can go with tiredness, feeling cold, weight gain or constipation. The doctor decides what it means with T3 and T4.
Urgent symptoms: a very fast or irregular heartbeat, chest pain, or high fever with confusion.

## T3 and T4
aliases: T3, T4, Free T3, Free T4, FT3, FT4, Thyroxine, Triiodothyronine
T3 and T4 are hormones made by the thyroid gland. They are read together with TSH.
If low: A low value can go with tiredness, feeling cold or weight gain. The doctor reads it with TSH.
If high: A high value can go with a racing heart, sweating, shaking or weight loss. The doctor reads it with TSH.
Urgent symptoms: a very fast or irregular heartbeat, chest pain, or high fever with confusion.

## Vitamin D
aliases: Vitamin D, 25-OH Vitamin D, 25 Hydroxy Vitamin D
This test measures the level of vitamin D in the blood, which helps the body use calcium for bones and muscles.
If low: A low value can go with bone or muscle aches and weakness. The doctor decides what, if anything, needs to be done.
If high: A high value is usually linked with taking too much vitamin D. The doctor decides what to do.
Urgent symptoms: severe bone pain after a minor fall, or persistent vomiting with confusion.

## Vitamin B12
aliases: Vitamin B12, B12, Cobalamin
This test measures the level of vitamin B12 in the blood. B12 is needed for healthy blood and nerves.
If low: A low value can go with tiredness, tingling or numbness in the hands or feet, or memory and mood changes. The doctor decides what needs to be done.
If high: A high value is often linked with supplements or other medical reasons. The doctor reviews it.
Urgent symptoms: difficulty walking, severe numbness, confusion, or chest pain.

## Ferritin and iron
aliases: Ferritin, Serum Iron, Iron, TIBC, Transferrin Saturation
Ferritin and iron tests show how much iron the body has in store and in the blood. They are read together with hemoglobin.
If low: A low value can go with tiredness, hair loss, feeling cold or breathlessness. The doctor decides what needs to be done.
If high: A high value is reviewed by a doctor, who looks for the reason.
Urgent symptoms: chest pain, severe breathlessness or fainting.

## Sodium and potassium
aliases: Sodium, Potassium, Na, K, Chloride, Electrolytes
Sodium, potassium and chloride are minerals in the blood that help nerves and muscles work and keep the body's fluid balance.
If low: A low value can cause headache, nausea, muscle weakness or cramps, and in more serious cases confusion. Low or high potassium can affect the heartbeat.
If high: A high value can cause thirst, weakness or confusion. High potassium can affect the heartbeat.
Urgent symptoms: palpitations, an irregular heartbeat, fainting, severe muscle weakness, confusion or fits.

## Calcium
aliases: Calcium, Serum Calcium
Calcium is a mineral in the blood that is important for bones, muscles and nerves.
If low: A low value can cause tingling around the mouth or in the fingers and muscle cramps.
If high: A high value can cause thirst, passing urine often, constipation, tiredness or confusion.
Urgent symptoms: muscle spasms, confusion, fits or an irregular heartbeat.

## C-reactive protein
aliases: CRP, C-Reactive Protein, hs-CRP
CRP is a protein that the body makes when there is inflammation somewhere in the body. It does not show where or why.
If low: A low value is usually not important by itself.
If high: A raised value shows that something may be going on in the body, but not what. A doctor interprets it with the symptoms and other tests.
Urgent symptoms: persistent high fever, severe pain, or trouble breathing.
